Patrice Lumumba rose from colonial obscurity to become the first Prime Minister of an independent Congo and a defining figure of the decolonization era. As leader of the Mouvement National Congolais, his uncompromising independence speech in 1960 galvanized Congolese nationalism and signaled that political sovereignty would not come with submission to former colonial powers. When the country descended into mutiny, foreign intervention, and secession, he fought to preserve unity, appealing internationally for support and forcing the Congo onto the global Cold War stage. He was removed from power and executed in 1961 with the involvement of domestic rivals and foreign interests. His actions — and the manner of his death — turned him into a lasting symbol of resistance to colonial influence and a reference point in debates over African sovereignty and foreign intervention.
